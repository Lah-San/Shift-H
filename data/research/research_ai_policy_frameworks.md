**Definitions (s7):** *AI* — "An interdisciplinary field, dealing with models and systems for the performance of functions generally associated with human intelligence, such as reasoning and learning." *AI System* — "An engineered system that generates predictive outputs such as content, forecasts, **recommendations**, or decisions for a given set of human defined objectives or parameters without explicit programming" (ISO/IEC 22989:2022 via DGov). *AI Accountable Officer* — an Executive overseeing AI strategy and governance. A cover-recommendation agent is squarely an "AI System" (it emits recommendations).

### A2. WA Health Artificial Intelligence Standard (Sept 2025, v1.1 Feb 2026)

Key content beyond the policy:

- **s5 What is AI:** learning/reasoning/adapting systems; RPA, OCR, statistical packages and "other automated decision-making systems" are not AI *per se*, but "WA health entities are asked to apply this AI Standard to other automated decision-making systems with an elevated risk". Automated decision-making is subject to the **Privacy and Responsible Information Sharing (PRIS) Act 2024**.
- **s6.1 Acceptable use (staff must):** only use AI systems that have been *assessed and approved*; never use AI to negatively impact a person's rights, safety or autonomy; use WA health information only as authorised by the **Data Custodian**; safeguard personal information; **disclose that AI has/will be used if results could impact people or decisions**; avoid over-reliance — "Staff remain accountable and responsible for making final decisions. AI tools should support and add value to work, rather than replace"; scrutinise outputs for inaccuracies, bias, hallucinations and report concerns; complete training; **retain records created by the AI system under the State Records Act 2000**; understand Gen-AI limitations.
- **s6.2 Clinical uses:** approval via a clinical governance body; final decision by a qualified clinician. (Leave-cover is a workforce, not clinical, use — but roster outputs affect patient safety via staffing, so patient-safety risk assessment still applies, see s8 P5.)
- **s7.2 Governance:** comply with WA Gov AI Policy & Assurance Framework; AI Accountable Officer; align with existing clinical safety, ethics, digital, ICT, information and research governance; ensure expertise for approvers.
- **s7.3 Workforce capability:** training on general AI, ethical/legal use, interpreting AI insights, validation, governance, risk, regulatory change.
- **s7.4 Risk and assurance:** DGov self-assessment results must be *approved by an Authorised Officer*, given to governance bodies, and *transferred to a risk register*; documented monitoring/evaluation plans; **feedback channels for users** and awareness of incident policies (MP 0122/19 Clinical Incident Management, MP 0135/20 Information Breach); legal advice where compliance concerns exist.
- **s7.5 Contract management:** vendors must declare AI in products, notify new AI features, allow opt-out, give ongoing assurance, and clarify liabilities.
- **s8 Ethics principles — health-specific mandatory content (most relevant to a workforce agent):**
  - P1 Wellbeing: documented purpose, benefits, efficacy, datasets, flaws and limitations, reported to governance; stakeholder input (clinicians, SMEs, legal); no deception, manipulation or undisclosed surveillance.
  - P2 Human-centred values: "the AI system is **not used to make unilateral decisions** that impact patients or citizens, or their human rights"; "humans always remain in control"; ethical review where appropriate; comply with Code of Conduct (MP 0124/19).
  - P3 Fairness: algorithms "must be impartial to prevent bias against individuals or groups based on **race, sex, gender, religion, culture, language, ethnicity** or any other form of discrimination"; outputs "continuously monitored for compliance with anti-discrimination laws and so that they do not disproportionately impact certain demographic groups"; staff trained to monitor bias.
  - P4 Privacy & security: MP 0015/16 Information Access Use & Disclosure; **no Official/Official: Sensitive/personal info into public Gen-AI**; de-identification/encryption; vendor data handling agreed by Information Custodian; **IPP 10 (Automated Decision Making) of PRIS Act 2024** applies where ADM uses personal information and makes significant decisions about individuals; security risk assessment (MP 0067/17) and cloud assessment (MP 0140/20).
  - P5 Reliability & safety: trained on fit-for-purpose data; tested with local, de-identified or synthetic data; **piloted if residual risk is moderate or higher**; monitoring plans that detect performance drift and biased results; independent audits if warranted; clear incident reporting; NIST AI RMF / AS ISO/IEC 23894 for complex or high-risk cases; ICT Patient Safety Risk Assessment if patients could be affected (staffing outputs can be).
  - P6 Transparency & explainability: only employ models that "can be explained and is understandable"; always be open when AI could significantly impact a person; Gen-AI disclosure statements must state how AI contributed (prompts, resources), the human oversight/editing applied, and the model version/type; **IPP 10 duties: notify the individual that ADM was used, explain on request how ADM is employed, and provide a process to request human intervention.**
  - P7 Contestability: "efficient and transparent review mechanism… must have human oversight".
  - P8 Accountability: named roles — AI Accountable Officer, Executive Sponsor, Accountable Supplier, Information Custodian, Authorised Officer, Technical Oversight, AI Model Validator, Independent AI Auditor, users/end users. **Records management:** all records created by or through AI kept under the State Records Act 2000 in the approved recordkeeping system, covering design, testing, implementation, evaluation and decommissioning.
- Appendix A checklist (clinical proposals): scope check → TGA → production vs research → submit WA Gov AI Assurance self-assessment, cloud assessment, patient-safety risk assessment, Pre-CAR/digital demand form → Information Governance approval if Official/personal info → Aboriginal & Health Equity impact statements (MP 0160/21, MP 0185/24) → AI Advisory Board where thresholds met.

### A3. WA Government AI Policy and AI Assurance Framework (Office of Digital Government, DPC)

- Hub: https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework (first published 5 Mar 2024, last updated 25 Aug 2026)
- Policy PDF: https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf (published Mar 2024, updated June 2025; based on NSW and DISR material)
- Self-assessment template: https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-06/selfassessmenttemplate.docx ; online form forms.digital.wa.gov.au/250231755970861
- Governance toolkit: https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-the-premier-and-cabinet/office-of-digital-government/artificial-intelligence-governance-toolkit-wa-public-sector-entities

**Policy principles (must be considered across design/data, development/validation, deployment, monitoring):** Human, social and environmental wellbeing; Human-centred values; Fairness ("Measures must be taken to ensure AI produced decisions are compliant with anti-discrimination laws"); Privacy protection and security (PRIS Act 2024, Privacy Act 1988; privacy by design); Reliability and safety; Transparency, explainability and contestability ("So affected stakeholders can know how the AI model reached its decision"; "In the case of decisions significantly affecting rights, there must be an effective system of oversight, which makes appropriate use of human judgment"); Accountability ("AI-based functions and decisions **must always be subject to human review and intervention**"; nominate an AI Accountable Officer); **Accuracy** (an additional WA principle: "Have the outputs been checked to ensure that they are factual, reliable and unbiased"). Also: consider whether a non-AI solution could achieve the same result.

**Assurance Framework (self-assessment) mechanics:**
- Mandatory "for any system or project that is driven by AI or automated decision making" (includes rules-based automation and generic AI platforms trained on agency data). Re-assess at: design/planning, end of pilot, pre-go-live, post roll-out, before major change.
- *Innovation pathway* exemption only if: no personal/sensitive data; data on Australian local servers/tenancy; **outputs do not inform any operations or decision making**; no external AI software procured or on free trial. (A hackathon demo on synthetic data can use this pathway; a pilot on real staff data cannot.)
- Controls checklist: decisions explainable; outputs subject to human validation; users trained; stakeholders consulted; independent/ethical review; **AI can be contested and is not the single source of truth**; regular audits for accuracy/fairness/bias; incident response plan; review mechanism for impacted people; model update/retraining process; ability to disengage.
- **Risk tiers:** residual risks rated N/A / **Low / Medium / High** on a likelihood × consequence matrix across three domains — (1) community benefit & fairness (discrimination, bias, harm to rights/entitlements), (2) influencing harmful decision-making (over-reliance; poor-quality inputs/outputs), (3) privacy & security (misuse, reputational, data location).
- **Step 1 "high risk in nature" categories** (automatic Board referral): biometrics; education/vocational access; law enforcement/justice; **"Employment, personnel management and access to self-employment (including recruitment, contracts and performance evaluation)"**; critical-infrastructure safety; **access to essential services including healthcare and emergency responses**. A leave-cover agent is *personnel management* and indirectly affects healthcare access → it is a high-risk-in-nature use case.
- Outcomes: all-low → manage locally; medium/high residual risk, offshore sensitive data, or high-risk category → referral to the **WA Government AI Advisory Board** (non-binding recommendations; project may continue during review). Other Board thresholds: Digital Capability Fund money; cost > $5 M. Board pack: detailed risk assessment, solution architecture, functional/non-functional requirements, business case, Privacy Impact Assessment, governance/change/training docs.
- Every entity must nominate an **AI Accountable Officer** who approves self-assessments.

### A4. National framework for the assurance of AI in government (Data and Digital Ministers, 21 June 2024) and Australia's AI Ethics Principles

- https://www.finance.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-06/National-framework-for-the-assurance-of-AI-in-government.pdf ; principles: https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/australias-artificial-intelligence-ethics-framework/australias-ai-ethics-principles
- Five cornerstones: **governance, data governance, risk-based approach, standards (AS ISO/IEC 42001, 23894, 38507), procurement.**

### A5. Translation into design requirements for a leave-cover recommendation agent

1. **Humans decide.** The agent produces *recommendations* to the roster manager/NUM; it must never auto-approve/deny leave or auto-assign cover. UI language: "Recommended", "Requires approval by …". (AI Standard s6.1, s8 P2/P8; WA Gov Policy Accountability.)
2. **Reasons on every output.** Each recommendation or "cannot accommodate" must list the rule(s) applied (with citations), the data inputs used, the alternatives considered, and a confidence/limitation note. (WA Gov Policy Transparency; National 6.3; Standard P6.)
3. **Disclose AI use to affected staff.** The requester and any staff proposed for cover must be told an AI tool contributed; a disclosure statement (how AI contributed, human oversight, model version) must accompany decisions. (Standard s6.1, P6; IPP 10 PRIS Act.)
4. **Contest / human intervention path.** Provide a one-click "request human review" and record it; align with ANF cl.33(1)(f) dispute path and cl.73. (Standard P7; IPP 10.)
5. **No protected attributes.** Features must exclude race, sex/gender, age, religion, culture, language, ethnicity, disability, pregnancy/parental status, union membership, and proxies (e.g., name, surname origin, postcode used as ethnicity proxy). Eligibility must rest on classification, competencies/credentials, contracted hours, availability, fatigue-rule compliance, cost order. Log fairness metrics (distribution of overtime/extra-shift offers by demographic groups, monitored offline by an authorised analyst). (Standard P3; Equal Opportunity Act 1984 (WA); Commonwealth anti-discrimination Acts listed in Standard App. B.)
6. **Data governance.** Use only roster/HR data authorised by the Data Custodian; no personal data to public Gen-AI; hosting in Australia; cloud assessment (MP 0140/20) and security assessment (MP 0067/17); Privacy Impact Assessment; keep leave *reasons* (medical, family violence) out of the model unless strictly needed and then de-identified. (Standard P4.)
7. **Records.** Persist every recommendation, inputs snapshot, model version, prompt/rules version, human decision and overrides as a State Records Act record in the approved system; retain audit trail. (Standard s6.1, P8; National 6.2.)
8. **Registration & approval before use.** Complete the DGov self-assessment (expect "high-risk in nature" → Board referral unless purely sandboxed), Authorised Officer approval, entry in the risk register, AI Accountable Officer sign-off, and vendor clauses if an external LLM is used. (Policy 3.4/3.6; Standard 7.4/7.5.)
9. **Monitoring and incidents.** Define acceptance criteria (e.g., % recommendations accepted, fatigue-rule violation rate = 0, ratio/NHpPD compliance), drift detection, periodic audits, feedback channel, and incident reporting via Datix/information-breach processes; ability to switch off. (Standard P5; National 5.4/5.5.)
10. **Training.** Roster managers must be trained on the tool's limits before use. (Policy 3.5.)
11. **Accuracy check.** Outputs must be validated against the source-of-truth roster (RoStar/HRMIS) before display; hallucinated staff/credentials must be impossible by construction (retrieve-then-reason, no free-text names). (WA Gov Policy Accuracy.)

## Part C — Order of preference for filling a vacant shift; fatigue/safe-hours rules

### C1. ANF Industrial Agreement 2024 (system-wide, binding)

**Clause 16 – Permanency of Employment and Relief Cover** (the closest thing to a system-wide "order of preference"):
- 16(1)(a)–(c): direct and permanent employment preferred; "fixed term contracts, casual employment, agency engagements and other contract for service arrangements are **not** the preferred methods".
- 16(1)(f): "**The Employer commits that all leave will ordinarily be covered**, provided that nothing … requires the Employer to provide leave cover where it can be demonstrated that such cover is not operationally necessary in a particular case."
- 16(1)(g): each hospital will "(i) employ **permanent relief staff** or ensure sufficient FTE are utilised for planned annual leave and planned ADO relief … and (ii) **maintain a pool of Casual Employees** to be utilised for short term work requirements."
- 16(2)(a): **agency only** if (i) no other suitably qualified employees available in the short term; (ii) bona fide emergency/urgent requirement; (iii) skills unavailable internally; or (iv) business-realignment reasons. 16(2)(b): before engaging agency, consider whether permanent **Surplus Employees** can do the work; agency duties reassessed every 3 months.
- 16(3)(b): fixed-term contracts allowed for backfill of leave/ADOs where a relief pool is impractical.
- Derived hierarchy (cl.16 + cl.14 + cl.30): (0) confirm cover is operationally necessary → (1) existing FTE / permanent relief pool / redeploy surplus staff at ordinary rates → (2) part-time staff additional hours (paid at ordinary rate up to 38 h/week or full-time shift length, cl.30(2)-(3)(d)) → (3) casual pool (25% loading, cl.14(1)(a)) → (4) overtime (1.5× first 3 h then 2×; 2× Sunday; 2.5× public holiday, cl.30(3)) → (5) agency (cl.16(2)).

**Clause 28 – Hours of Work and Rostering (fatigue/safe-hours limits):**
- 28(2) ordinary hours average 38/week (40 worked with ADO accrual); 28(10) **max 8 ordinary hours/day**, no broken shifts; 10-hour nights by agreement.
- 28(11) **20 hours free** when changing between night and day duty; 28(12) **9.5 hours** between evening and day duty.
- 28(13) full-timers not to work a combination of all night/day/evening in one roster week.
- 28(14) **≥2 full days off per week or 4 per fortnight**, consecutive where practicable, not preceded by a night shift.
- 28(15) **no more than 7 consecutive duties** (unless employee requests and employer approves); **no more than 10 duties per fortnight**; 10-hour nights: **max 5 consecutive**, **max 8 per fortnight**.
- 28(18) 14-day roster posted **28 days** in advance (min 14 in extenuating circumstances); roster may be altered for sickness/emergency, but working a rostered day off is paid as overtime; 28(19) notify employee of changes.
- 28(24)-(26) permanent night-shift staff: consultation, reasons, 4 weeks' notice, max 2 weeks/year of directed day/afternoon shifts.
- **Clause 30 – Overtime:** must be *authorised*; 30(4) **9.5-hour break** after overtime, else released without loss of pay, or paid double time until released; meal provisions.
- **Clause 31 – On call/recall:** min 3 h at overtime rate; 9.5-h break after recall; 31(16) **no on-call while on leave, the day before leave, or on/before ADOs** unless mutually agreed.
- **Clause 32 – Breaks:** 30-min meal after ≤5 h (5.5 h on 10-h shift; 6 h if start ≤0700); two 10-min paid refreshment breaks.
- **Clause 33 – Annual leave (approval rules):** 20 days/yr (+1 week continuous shift, +5 days on-call, +1 week north of 26°S); **employer must respond within 14 days**; **must not unreasonably refuse**; refusal **in writing with reasons within 14 days**; where "operational requirements" is cited, **details must be given**; disputes via cl.73; 33(16) leave in one block or two blocks ≥2 weeks after 12 months, portions ≥1 day by agreement; 33(20) accrued leave >10 weeks taken at employer convenience (min 5 days) except where a request was refused in the preceding 12 months.
- **Clause 38 – Personal leave:** 15 days/yr; "Reasonable and legitimate requests… will be approved subject to available credits" (illness, carer's, unanticipated/planned matters).
- **Clause 11 – Workload management:** employer to ensure staff "do not work excessive or unreasonable hours" and "can take leave provided for in this Agreement"; written workload grievance grounds include regular unpaid overtime and unfair burden due to skill mix; employer must explain mitigation strategies (in writing within 24 h on request).
- **Clause 10 – Ratios:** 4-stage escalation; coordinator excluded in principle.
