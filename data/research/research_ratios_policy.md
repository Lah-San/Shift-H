## 1. Policy identity and scope

- **Title:** Nurse/Midwife to Patient Ratios Policy. **Number:** MP 0187/24. **Effective from:** 30 September 2024. **Amended on:** 16 February 2026 (v2.0). **Review date:** January 2027. Approved by Sash Tomson, A/Assistant Director General, Strategy and Governance Division, 24 September 2024 (S2 ss.9–10).
- **Legal basis:** "This policy is a mandatory requirement for HSPs under the Clinical Services and Planning Policy Framework pursuant to section 26(2)(c) of the Health Services Act 2016." (S2 s.1)
- **Definition of ratios:** "Nurse/Midwife to Patient Ratios (ratios) are the proportion of nurses/midwives working on a ward or unit in relation to the number of patients under their care." (S2 s.1)
- **Industrial alignment:** the policy aligns with the ANF Agreement 2024 and the UWU Agreement 2024; "WA Health is committed to transitioning to the ratios model across the WA health system." (S2 s.1). ANF Agreement cl.10(1): "The Employer commits to introducing minimum nurse/midwife to patient ratios via a phased approach over a minimum of two years (WA Ratio Model)." cl.10(3): "The WA Ratio Model will replace Nursing Hour per Patient Day (NHpPD) at Part 2 of the Agreement." (S11)
- **Phasing / who is bound:** "Ratios will be implemented in a phased approach. Only specified metropolitan wards are bound by this policy. All other clinical care areas remain under the Nursing Hours per Patient Day methodology until their transition phase commences, at which time this policy will be updated accordingly." (S2 s.1)
- **Applicability (s.2):** "specified wards in the following metropolitan HSPs: Child and Adolescent Health Service. East Metropolitan Health Service. North Metropolitan Health Service. South Metropolitan Health Service." WA Country Health Service (WACHS) is **not** in scope — country sites remain on NHpPD.
- **Not applicable to:** "Assistants in Nursing (AINs). Nurses/midwives not delivering direct care to patients, with the exception of the hands-free shift coordinator (HFSC)." (S2 s.1)

## 2. The ratios — exact values by classification and shift (S3 Table 1; S7 Step 1)

Only **five ward classifications** currently exist in the WA Ratio Model. The policy itself contains no ratio numbers; they live in the mandatory related document *Wards/Units in Scope for Implementation* (S3), which "will be updated regularly".

| Classification | AM (morning) | PM (afternoon) | Night | HFSC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Medical and Surgical** | **1:4 + 1 HFSC** | **1:4 + 1 HFSC** | **1:7** (no HFSC required for compliance) | AM and PM only |
| **Coronary Care** | **1:2 + 1 HFSC** | **1:2 + 1 HFSC** | **1:3 + 1 HFSC** | all three shifts |
| **Intensive Care** | 1:1, OR 1:2 for lower acuity patients + 1 HFSC | same | same | all three shifts |
| **Neonatal Intensive Care** | 1:1, OR 1:2 for lower acuity NICU patients + 1 HFSC | same | same | all three shifts |
| **Paediatric Critical Care** | 1:1, OR 1:2 for lower acuity ICU patients + 1 HFSC | same | same | all three shifts |

Critical care caveat (S2 s.3.3): "In critical care areas, HSPs must consider the need for additional staffing as per the Australian College of Critical Care Nursing (ACCCN) Workforce Standards."

**Emergency departments:** the PCH ED pilot (July 2023) "saw rostered staffing levels lift to one nurse for every three patients" (Minister Sanderson, S13). ANMJ (S16) states "Emergency departments: 1 nurse to 3 patients". **However, no ED appears in the current Wards/Units in Scope table (S3), and no ED classification exists in the policy — treat 1:3 ED as [NOT VERIFIED AGAINST PRIMARY DOCUMENT] / not yet a mandatory ratio.** EDs otherwise remain on the NHpPP (per-presentation) formula (S8 s.10).

**Not covered by any ratio (remain on NHpPD):** mental health inpatient, rehabilitation, acute medical units not classified Medical and Surgical, PACU/recovery, theatres, maternity/postnatal/birth suite, general paediatric wards not in S3, palliative care, aged care/care-awaiting-placement, HDU as a standalone class, all WACHS sites. See section 8 for their NHpPD values.

## 3. Who counts toward the ratio (S2 ss.3.2.2, 3.3, 3.3.1; S11 cl.10(11); S7 p.31)

**Counted (direct-care headcount):**
- "The ratio must only be applied to enrolled nurses, registered nurses, and midwives." (S2 s.3.2.2)
- "HSPs must calculate the ratio by only using enrolled nurses, registered nurses, and midwives who provide direct patient care for the entirety of their shift." (S2 s.3.3.1)
- ANF cl.10(11): "the WA Ratio Model will include only registered nurses, midwives and enrolled nurses" (EN = Division 2 registrant).
- Employment type is not a criterion: nothing in the policy distinguishes permanent, casual, agency, pool or fixed-term staff; an EN/RN/midwife of any employment type providing direct care for the full shift counts. (Under NHpPD the same principle is explicit: hours "provided by permanent, temporary, casual, agency and pool nurses" count — S8 s.6.) Graduate RNs/ENs are registrants and are not excluded by the policy. [Inference from text; the withdrawn FAQ is not available to confirm any graduate/agency-specific rule.]

**Not counted:**
- "AINs must not be included in the ratio." (S2 s.3.2.2) — Assistants in Nursing / nursing assistants.
- **Hands-free shift coordinator:** "The HFSC is not counted in the ratio and must not be included in the number of nurses/midwives providing direct care." (S2 s.3.3). HFSC = "Suitably senior registered nurse or midwife who oversees a ward/unit for the entirety of their shift in a supernumerary capacity." (S2 s.7). "The HFSC must be a suitably senior clinician as determined by the HSP." The ANF Agreement cl.10(7)(d): the model will "in principle, exclude the coordinator from the ratio".
- **Supernumerary roles:** "Roles that are not providing direct patient care for the entirety of the shift are supernumerary to the ratio and must not be used when calculating the ratio." (S2 s.3.3.1). Definition: "supernumerary Nurses and Midwives are those not counted in the ratio due to not spending an entire shift providing direct patient care." (S2 s.7)
- WPF (S7 p.31) lists the indirect roles outside the ratio: "Hands-Free Shift Co-Ordinator ... Nurse Unit Managers, Midwife Managers, Staff Development Nurses, Clinical Nurse Specialists, Clinical Midwife Specialists, Clinical Nurse Consultants, Clinical Midwife Managers, Area Managers." and states "Minimum ratios do not include indirect hours."
- Students: not employees, not registrants — cannot count. (NHpPD S8: supernumerary "scheduled and supervised practice ... not counted in the staff roster profile".)
- Withdrawn FAQ snippet [NOT VERIFIED]: "additional supernumerary nursing/midwifery roles such as those related to leadership and education, as well as specialty roles such as resuscitation sit outside of NMTPR and are to be staffed as deemed necessary."

**Partial-shift direct care:** a CNS or NUM who takes a patient load for half a shift does not count ("for the entirety of their shift").

## 4. Skill mix and in-charge rules

- "3.3.3 Skill Mix — HSPs must use clinical judgement to determine the required skill mix within the ratio based on the demand and acuity of the ward/unit." (S2). WPF: "The policy does not prescribe a skill mix, which is left to the discretion of clinical judgment at a local level." (S7 p.5). **There is no numeric RN:EN minimum in the policy.**
- **In-charge requirement:** the HFSC (an RN or midwife, "suitably senior") is mandatory on AM and PM shifts on Medical and Surgical wards and on all three shifts in the four critical-care classifications (S3). On Medical/Surgical night shift: "HSPs must consider if a HFSC in addition to the minimum ratio is clinically necessary on night shift, where it is specified ... that a HFSC on night shift is not required for the purposes of ratios compliance." (S2 s.3.3). Withdrawn FAQ snippet [NOT VERIFIED]: "Night shifts may staff a shift coordinator should this be current practice or if determined clinically appropriate, however this is not required for the purposes of NMTPR compliance."
- ANF cl.11(4)(f) makes it a valid workload grievance if team members are "consistently placed under an unreasonable or unfair burden or lack of adequate professional guidance because of the workload or the staffing skill mix of the team."

## 5. Calculation mechanics (S2 ss.3.3.2, 3.4.1; S7)

- **Rounding:** "When calculating ratios, HSPs must ensure the staffing number for the ward/unit is rounded up to the nearest whole number." (S2 s.3.3.2). Required direct-care staff = ceil(patients ÷ ratio denominator) + HFSC where required. Examples: 29 patients on a Med/Surg AM shift → ceil(29/4)=8 + 1 HFSC = 9; night → ceil(29/7)=5. 30 patients → 8+1 AM, 5 night. 22 patients → 6+1 AM, 4 night. A CCU with 9 patients AM → 5 + HFSC; night → 3 + HFSC.
- **Ward-level, not individual-level:** "HSPs must ensure that the overall ratio for a unit/ward is met on all shifts. This does not preclude individual Nurses/Midwives from carrying an individual patient load that is below or above the ratio, as long as the overall ratio at a ward/unit level is met." (S2 s.3.4.1)
- **Minimum, not target:** "Individual wards/units may be staffed above the ratio if determined to be clinically required." (S2 s.3.2.1). Requests to staff *above* the ratio category go through the Service Profile Executive Summary to the CNMO (S2 s.3.8).
- **Newborns as patients:** ANF cl.10(7)(e): the model will "count qualified babies as patients consistent with the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare newborn qualification status and babies where clinically indicated". (Unqualified newborns ≤9 days rooming-in with mother are not patient days — S8 Table 2.)
- **Occupancy drives staffing:** the WPF roster construct is built on "bed capacity, average occupancy, and historical data on demand/acuity" (S7 p.28); worked example: 30-bed Med/Surg ward, 97% occupancy = 29 occupied beds → "8 nurses + HFSC required for AM/PM shift and 5 nurses required for ND shift" (S7 p.35). Direct-care staff work "8 hour day shifts and 10 hour night shifts". Occupancy formula: "Average Occupancy Rate = (Total Occupied Bed Days ÷ Total Available Bed Days) × 100" (S7 p.33). Actual shift compliance is measured against actual patients on the shift, so occupancy above the roster-construct assumption ("Occupancy over profile" — S4) is a recognised cause of non-compliance.

## 6. When the ratio cannot be met — non-compliance, escalation, acceptable cover

### 6.1 Definition and reporting (S2 s.3.6)
- "A non-compliant shift is one that does not staff at sufficient level to meet the minimum relevant ratio, and/or a shift that does not staff a HFSC when required."
- **Grace period:** "The four months immediately following a ward/unit's transition to ratios is known as the grace period. Non-compliance reporting will not be required in this period."
- "HSPs must report all instances of ratios non-compliance as soon as practicable following the end of the non-compliant shift using the Nurse/Midwife to Patient Ratios Non-Compliance Form."
- "The non-compliance form must be escalated to the relevant HSP Steering Committee. The Steering Committee must review the form and determine an outcome within six (6) weeks of the non-compliant shift concluding." (extended from four weeks in v2.0)
- HSPs must have a local procedure for escalating non-compliance and record keeping.

### 6.2 The Non-Compliance Form (S4) — the policy's own taxonomy of causes and cover
"The Nurse Manager (or equivalent) must complete the relevant sections of this form detailing the instance of non-compliance and identify the mitigation strategy implemented."

Reason for non-compliance (tick list):
1. "Known roster shortage unable to be replaced. Shortfall unfilled."
2. "Unplanned personal leave, unable to be replaced. Shortfall unfilled."
3. "Occupancy over profile."
4. "Staff resource balanced to another clinical area for higher clinical need."
5. "Reallocation of resources for high acuity / specials."
6. "Other."

Mitigation strategy (to be chosen "After discussion with Clinical Nurse Specialist and/or Shift Coordinator"):
1. "Identified the current capacity and capability of the Nursing resources and reallocated resources as required."
2. "Changed/altered patient/consumer activity/demand where possible."
3. "Changed/altered Nursing skill mix where possible."
4. "Sought assistance from Nursing support services to seek additional staffing."
5. "Escalated the identified imbalance(s) through the governance framework."
6. "Other (provide detail)."

Steering Committee outcome: "Mitigations strategy noted and supported" or "Additional actions required". ToR (S6 s.3.2): "Should the Steering Committee elect to not approve the form, an alternative mitigation strategy must be developed and returned to the relevant ward/unit for action. In instances of repeated non-compliance, the Steering Committee must work directly with the relevant ward/unit to develop effective mitigation strategies."

### 6.3 Acceptable cover hierarchy (industrial agreement, S11)
The policy does not rank cover types; the ANF Agreement does:
- cl.16(1)(f): "The Employer commits that all leave will ordinarily be covered, provided that nothing in this Agreement requires the Employer to provide leave cover where it can be demonstrated that such cover is not operationally necessary in a particular case."
- cl.16(1)(g): each Hospital will "(i) employ permanent relief staff or ensure sufficient full time equivalent (FTE) are utilised for planned annual leave and planned accrued day off (ADO) relief requirements in accordance with Hospital activity; and (ii) maintain a pool of Casual Employees to be utilised for short term work requirements."
- cl.16(1)(a)-(b): "direct employment is the preferred form of engagement; ... permanent employment is the preferred mode"; "fixed term contracts, casual employment, agency engagements ... are not the preferred methods".
- cl.16(2)(a): "Agency engagements are not the preferred method of delivery of services and will only be used: (i) if there are no other suitably qualified employees available in the short term; (ii) if there is a bona fide emergency or urgent work requirement; (iii) if the skills required cannot be obtained internally in the short term; ..."
- cl.16(3)(b): fixed-term contracts may be used "(i) to cover one-off periods of relief, including backfill for temporary absences ... absence on leave"; "(vii) annual leave where staffing arrangements are such that it is impractical to maintain a pool of staff to provide annual leave cover".
- cl.28(18): "A roster may be altered at any time to enable the nursing/midwifery service of the hospital to be carried on where another employee is absent from duty on account of illness, or if the hospital emergencies render any alternative necessary, provided that where such alteration involves an employee working on their rostered day off, other than an ADO, the employee will be paid ... in accordance with clause 30 - Overtime."
- cl.30 Overtime: time-and-a-half first 3 hours then double time Mon–Sat; double time Sunday; double-time-and-a-half public holidays; cl.30(4) "Where overtime is worked there will be a break of 9.5 hours between shifts."
- Implied hierarchy for a cover agent: (1) permanent relief/pool FTE and internal redeployment (resource balancing within the HSP), (2) casual pool, (3) part-timers' additional hours / overtime, (4) fixed-term backfill for longer leave, (5) agency last.

### 6.4 Workload-concern escalation (S2 s.3.7; S5; S11 cl.10(9), 11(5))
Four-stage process (Part 2, cl.10(9) ANF Agreement; cl.12A(9) UWU Agreement):
- "Stage 1 – matter to be raised with the relevant line manager (which may include After Hours Manager/Coordinator) responsible for ensuring the ratio has been correctly applied, with parties to engage and resolve the concern within the shift."
- "Stage 2 – referral ... to the nominated nursing and midwifery executive (Nursing/Midwifery Director or higher)" — the form (S5) names the Coordinator of Nursing (CON), who informs the Co-Director.
- "Stage 3 – referral ... to the relevant health service provider/Hospital Steering Committee."
- "Stage 4 – referral of an unresolved Stage 3 dispute to the WAIRC." "Nothing will prevent either party from applying to the WAIRC at any stage."
- Timing: cl.10(10): the procedure "will only apply to the relevant ward/unit four months after the WA Health Ratio Model is implemented at that relevant ward/unit" (= grace period, mirrored in S2 s.3.7).
- Employer response obligations (cl.11(5)(c)): must "explain what strategies have been used and/or will be used to immediately mitigate the workload issue"; "such explanation may be provided verbally within the shift, but will be provided in writing within 24 hours if requested by the employee."
- **"Reasonable steps":** cl.11(2)-(3): "The Employer is committed to addressing workload management issues and taking reasonable steps to ensure employees are allocated sustainable workloads. The Employer will take reasonable steps to ensure employees (a) do not work excessive or unreasonable hours; and (b) can take leave provided for in this Agreement."

## 7. Ratios and leave planning / rostering

The policy contains **no explicit clause about leave**; the obligation is derived from combining s.3.2.1 ("HSPs must staff their wards/units at the minimum ratio"), s.3.4.1 ("overall ratio ... met on all shifts"), the Non-Compliance Form's cause list ("Known roster shortage unable to be replaced" — i.e. a roster published short is itself a reportable breach), and the industrial/HR instruments:

- **Rosters must be built to ratio.** WPF Step 7: rosters "should be based off the roster construct" whose per-shift numbers are "based on ratio and average occupancy" (S7 pp.35, 43). The construct is direct-care headcount per shift + HFSC.
- **Leave relief is funded and rostered separately from the ratio headcount.** WPF Step 4: non-productive hours (annual leave, personal leave, PDL, ADO, TOIL, LSL, parental, mandatory training) are converted to backfill FTE using local multipliers; "if the activity in which an employee is normally engaged (e.g. provision of clinical care) continues during the period of leave, replacement of that employee to the same level must occur during the leave period." (S7 p.39). Example multipliers (Award entitlements): Annual leave 4 weeks = 5.77%; additional 1 week for continuous shift workers = 1.15%; Personal leave 15 days = 7.7% (table also shows 4.23%, 4.61%, 1.92% for TOIL/ADO/PDL-type lines). FTE = annual hours ÷ 1976 (38 h × 52). 1.0 FTE = 76 h/fortnight.
- **Roster notice:** cl.28(18): "A 14 day roster will ordinarily be posted 28 days prior to the commencing date of the first working period in the roster. In extenuating circumstances ... not less than 14 days prior." ADO roster "posted at least four weeks before" (cl.28(3)(a)(ii)).
- **Leave approval rules (cl.33(1)):** "(b) The Employer will respond to an application for annual leave within 14 days ... (c) ... An Employer must not unreasonably refuse an employee's request for annual leave. (d) Where an Employer refuses ... notify the employee in writing and outline the reasons for the refusal within 14 days ... (e) Where the Employer provides 'operational requirements' as the reason for not approving the request, the Employer will provide details of those operational requirements." Disputes go to cl.73.
- Annual leave: 20 days/yr (+ up to 5 days for continuous shift workers; + 1 week north of 26th parallel); "in one continuous period or in two periods of not less than two weeks" (cl.33(16)), or by agreement "in multiple portions of not less than one day" (cl.33(18)); accrual >10 weeks may be directed "at the operational convenience of the Employer", minimum 5 days (cl.33(20)) unless the employee's leave was refused in the prior 12 months (cl.33(21)).
- **Roster construction constraints for the cover recommendation (cl.28):** max 8 ordinary hours/day (10-hour nights by agreement); "free from duty during the 20 hours immediately preceding" a change between night and day duty (cl.28(11)); 9.5 hours between evening and day duty (cl.28(12)); "not less than two full days [off] in each week or four full days in each fortnight" (cl.28(14)); "not ... more than seven consecutive duties" without the employee's request and approval; "No employee will be rostered to work more than ten duties over a fortnightly period"; max five consecutive 10-hour nights, max eight 10-hour nights per fortnight (cl.28(15)); permanent night-shift employees can only be directed onto day/afternoon shifts for max two weeks per year with 4 weeks' written notice (cl.28(24)-(26)).
- **Management of Accrued Leave Policy MP 0100/18 (S12):** managers must consider "organisational priorities; employee preference for holiday periods; fairness and equity"; employees must "provide management with sufficient notice of future leave requests, to enable adequate workforce planning"; entities must "Regularly assess staffing levels and leave patterns to maintain safe service delivery"; "Plan for peak periods or seasonal fluctuations in demand to take leave entitlements. This may include appropriately staffing departments in anticipation of both planned and unplanned leave."; consider "not backfilling a vacancy of less than two weeks" (excess-leave strategy — note: on a ratio ward, not backfilling is only permissible if the ratio is still met); "where regular backfill is required, consider engaging permanent relief staff". Annual leave must not exceed two entitlements.
- **What "adequate cover" means on a ratio ward:** after the leave is granted, every shift in the affected period must still have ≥ ceil(expected patients ÷ ratio) direct-care EN/RN/midwives who will be present for the whole shift, plus a suitably senior RN/midwife HFSC on each shift where required, without breaching the cl.28 hours/rest rules for anyone who is moved. Nothing in the policy allows an HFSC to be "counted down" into the ratio to cover a gap — doing so makes the shift non-compliant on the HFSC limb.

## 8. NHpPD — the predecessor methodology still governing out-of-scope wards (S8, S11 cl.12 & Schedule A)

- ANF cl.12(1): "This clause applies to areas in which the WA Ratio Model has not been applied." cl.12(3): the Employer manages workloads per the "Nurses (WA Government Health Services) Exceptional Matters Order 2001 (PR914193) (the EMO) relating to workloads (Nursing Hours per Patient Day)". EMO cl.6.1 required NHpPD "from no later than 1 April 2002". S8 s.1: "during the transition to the WA Ratio Model, HSPs will be using both the WA Ratio Model and NHpPD".
- S8 Table 3 — **NHpPD Guiding Principles (hours of direct nursing care per patient per day):**
  - **A = 7.5** — High complexity; HDU ≤6 beds within a ward; tertiary step-down ICU; high intervention level; specialist tertiary unit 1:2 staffing; tertiary paediatrics; MH secure beds (seclusion, high self-harm risk, intermittent 1:1/1:2 nursing, 15-minutely observations).
  - **B = 6.0** — High complexity, no HDU; tertiary step-down CCU/ICU; moderate/high intervention; ED observation ward; special unit incl. Mental Health Unit; high patient turnover >50%; secondary paediatrics; tertiary maternity; MH high risk (30-min obs, occasional 1:1).
  - **C = 5.75** — High complexity acute care unit/ward; moderate turnover >35% OR emergency admissions >50%; MH moderate risk, psychogeriatric MH unit, MH unit with ECT facility.
  - **D = 5.0** — Moderate complexity; acute rehabilitation secondary level; acute unit/ward; emergency admissions >40% OR moderate turnover >35%; secondary maternity; MH medium-low risk, forensic patients in open beds.
  - **E = 4.5** — Moderate complexity; moderate turnover >35%; sub-acute unit/ward.
  - **F = 4.0** — Rural paediatrics; rural maternity.
  - **G = 3.0** — Moderate/low complexity; low turnover <35%; care awaiting placement/aged care; sub-acute; MH slow-stream rehabilitation.
  - **ICU = 31.60**, **CCU = 14.16**, **HDU = 12.00** (Table 4; ICU shift coordinator requires ≥10 beds for 26 h, access nurse ≥8 beds for 24 h; HDU ≥6 beds for 16 h coordinator; non-direct roles excluded).
  - **Renal (T) = 3.02, Renal (S) = 2.18** hours per session.
  - **ED = NHpPP = B + (C × D) + E** per ATS category (ATS1: assessment 2.00 h, ongoing 1.00 h/h, additional roles 1.00; ATS2: 1.00/0.50/0.50; ATS3: 0.50/0.33/0.33; ATS4: 0.50/0.25/0.25; ATS5: 0.25/0.17/0.17). EDs >60,000 presentations/yr get a Shift Coordinator.
  - "Turnover = admissions + transfers + discharges divided by number of beds".
- **NHpPD formula (S8 Fig.1):** Nursing hours/day = NHpPD × average occupied beds; Total FTE/week = (nursing hours × days in roster period) ÷ 76 h. Example: category B, 30 beds, 96.6% occupancy (29 beds) → 6.0 × 29 = 174 h/day → 32.05 FTE per 14-day roster. Mixed wards are weighted by patient-cohort split (e.g. 67% C + 33% D = 5.5).
- **Who counts under NHpPD:** "direct productive hours provided by permanent, temporary, casual, agency and pool nurses"; excluded: Doze, mandatory training, orientation, other clinical area, supernumerary, non-productive (leave), non-direct roles (CNM/NUM/ANM/CNS/CMS/Area Manager/SDN). **"The Shift Coordinator is part of NHpPD and therefore is not excluded in any category."** (opposite of the ratio model's HFSC exclusion). Patient hours exclude boarders, unqualified newborns and patients on leave.
- **Compliance thresholds (S8 s.3):** brief explanation where a ward is "0 – 10% below their target NHpPD"; "detailed report, including actions taken to resolve the deficit, where a ward/unit is 10% or more below their target NHpPD". 6-monthly reports to CNMO.
- "NHpPD reclassification requests may not be submitted in the 6 months prior to transition to the WA Ratio Model." (S8 s.12). ANF cl.10(7)(b): where the audit finds the NHpPD level "provides the highest number of nurses or midwives per shift that prescribed staffing level will be retained".
- Maternity: Level 4–6 services apply NHpPD to postnatal wards only; Level 2–3 use a combined ward + labour/birth method; "NHpPD are not applied to Midwifery Group Practice models." (S8 s.9)
- **EMO Schedule A** duties still binding (cl.12(4)): "DUTY TO PREVENT SUSTAINED UNREASONABLE WORKLOAD" (cl.5), duty to allocate and roster per NHPPD with "clinical assessment of patient needs; the demands of the environment such as ward layout; statutory obligations ...; nurse regulatory legislation and professional standards; and reasonable workloads" (cl.6.3); exemption for "emergency or extra-ordinary circumstances of an urgent kind so long as such work is not work regularly added to the employee's weekly or daily roster" (cl.5 proviso).

## 9. Definitions (S2 s.7; S8 Table 2; S11)

- **Direct care:** "Care which involves any direct interaction with a patient, including treatments, counselling, self-care, patient education and administration of medication."
- **HFSC:** "Suitably senior registered nurse or midwife who oversees a ward/unit for the entirety of their shift in a supernumerary capacity."
- **Supernumerary:** "not counted in the ratio due to not spending an entire shift providing direct patient care."
- **Steering Committee:** "Health Service Provider level Steering Committee, established to provide local-level support and oversight for the effective implementation and monitoring of ratios." Must exist ≥1 month before ratios commence; meets "monthly at a minimum"; quorum 50%+1 (S6).
- **Shift:** not formally defined; the policy and forms use AM / PM / Night. WPF assumes 8-hour AM/PM and 10-hour night for direct-care staff (S7 p.35); ANF cl.28(10) caps ordinary hours at 8/day, 10-hour nights by agreement; 12-hour rosters only via cl.29 agreement.
- **Ward/unit:** the unit of compliance (S2 s.3.4.1); named per hospital in S3.
- **Patient:** admitted patients on the ward incl. qualified newborns (cl.10(7)(e)); NHpPD excludes boarders, unqualified newborns and patients on leave.
- **FTE:** ANF cl.3(9): "contracted for an average of 38 hours per week"; 1976 h/yr; 76 h/fortnight.
- **Continuous shift employee:** rostered for afternoon and/or night with day shift, or permanent nights over seven days (cl.33(8)).
- **Grace period:** four months after transition (S2 s.3.6).
- **Non-productive hours:** leave and mandatory training (S7 p.38; S8 Table 2).

## 10. Compliance monitoring and governance (S2 ss.3.5, 3.8, 4)

- Six-monthly report to CNMO of "the percentage of compliant and non-compliant shifts by ward/unit by month" and the six-month average; copies of local non-compliance and escalation procedures annually; initial Executive Summary within 6 months of transition, re-endorsed and resubmitted by **31 March** each year. Contact CNMO.CED@health.wa.gov.au.
- ToR (S6 s.3.1): Steering Committee monitors reporting and identifies "where actions such as ward reclassification may need to be undertaken".
